Business context

Why Aurum needs to transform

Aurum is a fictional 60-cover fine-dining restaurant. Its food is excellent, but its digital presence has not kept up. Bookings are taken only by phone, the menu is a PDF that is hard to read on a mobile, there is no way to collect feedback, and loyal guests are not rewarded.

As CTO, my brief is to use web technology to help Aurum compete with newer restaurants that already offer a seamless online experience.

Engagement

Social media integration, promotions and a loyalty scheme.

Efficiency

Structured online booking requests reduce phone time.

Experience

A fast, accessible, mobile-friendly menu and booking flow.

Promotion

Timed events and email marketing with clear consent.

        Privacy, security & data protection
        
How Aurum protects your data

        

          
UK GDPR principles
Aurum only asks for the data needed to handle a booking (data minimisation) and uses it only for that purpose. Marketing consent is a separate, unticked box, as UK GDPR and PECR require.

          
No data stored here
The form has no database. Details are passed to your own email app, so nothing is held on the website, reducing the risk of a data breach.

          
Cookies
A cookie notice appears on the first visit. Only your cookie choice is saved in your browser. No tracking or advertising cookies are set.

          
Secure by default
GitHub Pages serves the site over HTTPS. External links use rel="noopener noreferrer" to protect visitors, and input is validated before an email is built.

          
Your rights
Guests can ask Aurum to see, correct or delete their data at any time by emailing the reservations team. Booking emails are deleted after 12 months.

          
Ethical design
No dark patterns. Buttons say exactly what they do, and declining marketing never affects a booking.

        

      

    


    
    

      

        Design choices
        
Why this solution is built the way it is

        

          

            
The page follows a guest’s journey from discovery to booking, so every section moves the user closer to a reservation. A luxury palette of black and champagne gold matches Aurum’s fine-dining positioning, while generous spacing keeps the page calm and easy to scan.

            
I chose a client-side mailto approach rather than a paid booking platform because it meets the brief without a database, costs nothing to run and keeps personal data off the server. The trade-off is that staff still confirm bookings manually, so a future phase would integrate a booking API.
